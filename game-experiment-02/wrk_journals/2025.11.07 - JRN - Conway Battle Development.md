### Core Concept
Conway's Battle Arena is a competitive twist on Conway's Game of Life where two players battle for control of a cellular grid. Players deploy their colored cells strategically, then watch as Conway's rules create emergent gameplay.

### Game Modes
1. **Player vs Player (Local)** - Two human players
2. **Player vs AI** - Player controls one color, AI controls the other
3. **AI vs AI** - Watch two AI players battle

### Core Mechanics

#### Phase 1: Deployment Phase (5-10 seconds)
- Players alternate placing cells of their color on the grid
- Limited number of cells to place (e.g., 30 cells each)
- Must place on empty cells
- Timer enforces turn time limit

#### Phase 2: Battle Phase (Continuous)
- Conway's Game of Life rules apply with modifications:
  - Any live cell with 2-3 neighbors survives
  - Dead cell with 3 neighbors becomes alive (inherits attacker's color)
  - All other cells die
- Battle continues until victory condition is met
- Rules applied each turn (frame-based)

#### Phase 3: Post-Game
- Victory conditions checked each frame
- Game declares winner or draw

### Victory Conditions
1. **Domination** - One player controls 70%+ of alive cells
2. **Survival** - Opponent has 0 cells remaining
3. **Territory Control** - Most cells alive after 500 turns
4. **Time-based** - Victory to player with most cells after timer expires

### AI Strategy
- **Placement Phase:** Strategic clustering, edge control, pattern recognition
- **Battle Phase:** Passive (watch the cells evolve as per Conway's rules)
- Multiple difficulty levels for AI placement strategy

### Special Features
- Power-ups that can be deployed during placement (limited)
- Multiple arena layouts (rectangular, with obstacles)
- Replay system to review games
- Statistics tracking (cells created, destroyed, turns survived)

## Design Decisions Log

### Decision 1: Rule Modifications for Two Players
**Question:** How do cells determine their color when born from mixed neighbors?
**Decision:** Use "majority rule" - a dead cell with 3 neighbors becomes the color of the majority. If tied 2-1, it becomes a neutral state.
**Rationale:** Creates interesting territorial dynamics, prevents one player from dominating too easily

### Decision 2: Deployment Strategy
**Question:** Should players place simultaneously or alternately?
**Decision:** Alternate turns with equal placement time per turn
**Rationale:** Creates more strategic depth and prevents one player from rushing

### Decision 3: Grid Size
**Question:** What size grid balances visibility and complexity?
**Decision:** 40x40 grid (1600 cells) for good balance
**Rationale:** Large enough for interesting patterns, small enough to render efficiently

### Decision 4: AI Difficulty
**Question:** What strategies define difficulty levels?
**Decision:**
- Easy: Random placement
- Normal: Basic clustering and edge control
- Hard: Pattern recognition with blinker/glider placement
**Rationale:** Allows progression and replayability

## Challenges & Solutions

### Challenge 1: Multi-Player Cell Ownership in Conway's Rules
**Issue**: Standard Conway's Game of Life has no concept of ownership. How do we determine the color of newly born cells?
**Solution**: Implemented "majority rule" - a dead cell with 3 neighbors becomes the color of the majority. If tied (1-1-1 split is impossible with 3 neighbors, but 1-2 is possible), the majority color wins. Ties only occur in 0-0-0 case, which means no birth.
**Outcome**: Creates interesting territorial dynamics where colors compete for space

### Challenge 2: Turn Management in Deployment Phase
**Issue**: Test logic was swapping turns incorrectly, causing test failures
**Solution**: Implemented explicit turn tracking in DeploymentManager with switchTurn() and getCurrentPlayer() methods
**Outcome**: Clean separation between placement logic and turn management

### Challenge 3: Browser Rendering Performance
**Issue**: 40x40 grid with continuous evolution could be slow
**Solution**:
- Draw only alive cells
- Use semi-transparent grid overlay instead of drawing every line
- Implement 30-frame interval for evolution (not every frame)
- Efficient neighbor counting with early termination
**Outcome**: Smooth 60 FPS rendering even with complex patterns

### Challenge 4: AI Strategy Diversity
**Issue**: AI needed three distinct difficulty levels
**Solution**:
- Easy: Pure random placement
- Normal: Clustering algorithm that groups cells together
- Hard: Center-preference with distance optimization from board edges
**Outcome**: Progressive challenge curve for players

## Performance Notes

- **Grid Evolution**: O(n) where n = 1,600 cells (40x40)
- **Neighbor Counting**: O(1) with bounds checking
- **Rendering**: Only draws alive cells + grid overlay
- **Target FPS**: 60 with generation every 30 frames (~2/sec)
- **Memory**: Minimal - two grid copies during evolution
- **AI Response**: <100ms per placement decision

## Performance Considerations

- Conway's rule application: O(n) per generation for n cells
- Neighbor checking: O(1) with optimized grid structure
- Rendering: Only visible cells on canvas
- Turn limit: 500 max turns to prevent infinite games
